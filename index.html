Truck-loading robot

2024–2026. A CoreXY gantry with a vacuum gripper.

It’s built to pick up boxes with a suction cup and place them in a model truck bed so the load stays balanced. A Python program works out where each box should go, and an ESP32 drives the motors.

I welded the steel frame myself, built the control board, and wrote the firmware and the planner. My robotics teacher helped me with the code.

All-Russian School Olympiad, technology (robotics track): regional prize-winner and national finalist, 2025.

In summer 2026 I had to wipe my laptop and lost the newest firmware. Now I’m restoring it from the robot itself and an older backup.

Drawing robot

2025–2026. Built for the All-Russian School Olympiad.

It draws geometric shapes on a vertical sheet of paper. Two motors drive it along a gear rack, a third moves the pen along the arm on a lead screw, and a servo lifts the pen off the paper. I built it to the olympiad’s official technical spec for 2026 and rebuilt it once along the way.

All-Russian School Olympiad, informatics (robotics track): regional winner and national finalist, 2026.

Line follower

2024 to now.

My longest project. I built it once and keep rebuilding it, moving the sensors and motors around to see what works better.

Does the clever algorithm actually help?

The loader’s planner balances the load with simulated annealing. On the robot’s 20 boxes, a test of 30 runs showed it did no better than random swaps: both left the center of mass about 0.3 mm off.

My guess is that annealing only pays off with thousands of boxes. So I rewrote it in C++ and I’m testing that guess on up to 45,000 virtual boxes. I’ll post the results here in November.
